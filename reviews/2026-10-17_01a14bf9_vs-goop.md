## Lessons
1. **Time "draw until N" refills for when your hand is empty** ([H-1](#cite-h-1), refined four times in this game).
   - On the play with one card a turn, a 7-card hand runs out about turn 6.
   - The cap absorbs earlier draws, so drawing early buys timing and consistency, not extra cards.
   - Before a refill, spare cards are effectively free ink. But that doesn't apply to engines or must-answer threats (Dumbo).
   - Check the opponent's hand size before casting, because the refill feeds them too.
2. **Once later turns will be short of cards rather than ink, unspent ink isn't waste.** Don't spend a card just to use ink ([H-2](#cite-h-2)).
3. **Against Amber/Sapphire's Madrigal package, don't hand them damage they can heal.**
   - Chip damage and counter-damage become cards (Ohana, FIND THAT VISION) or lore (Isabela – Caring Cultivator).
   - Don't quest with a character whose counter-damage would become their lore.
   - Kill Isabela – Caring Cultivator first.
4. **Their main removal only hits characters with 5+ {S}.** Plan around which of your characters are exposed: here, Demona – Scourge, and Wasabi when you hold an ink drop.
5. **Respect Raging Storm once an Amber opponent reaches 8 ink, but don't freeze.** [Odds table](#cite-kb-meta-inks-raging-storm). Expect the wipe after their quests, not before.
6. **"Anything they play this turn can quest next turn."** Count every character they have, and every location with lore, when working out what must die.
7. **Quest first, then remove (or wipe),** and pay ink for songs rather than singing when your characters are worth more questing. Both players did this to good effect.
8. **Use repeatable effects as a reaction, not pre-emptively** ([H-3](#cite-h-3), open). The user thinks it's too general to promote yet.

> ### H-2. Spare ink isn't automatically wasted when later turns will be short of cards
> - **Claim:** Ink grows by one every turn, but cards don't. Once later turns are likely to have more ink than
>   good cards to spend it on, cards are the scarcer resource. Then playing a low-impact card just to use spare
>   ink (giving up a better card to the inkwell instead) is usually worse than leaving the ink unspent.
> - **Applies when:** you're ahead on board (POS-1) and the extra play has little impact (WIN-2). Also when the
>   backup value of the extra card is low, e.g. a second Shift target when the opponent's removal can't hit the first.
> - **Doesn't apply when:** you're behind and need board presence now, or the cheap card has real impact.
> - **Evidence for:** replay 01a14bf9 (vs Goop), turn 5. The user left 1 ink unspent rather than ink Cheshire to
>   play a second Mulan – Injured Soldier. Coach first called it a close call, then agreed after the user's argument.
> - **Evidence against:** none yet.
> - **Status:** open.

<a id="cite-h-1"></a>**H-1**: hypothesis, `kb/strategy/hypotheses.md` at `b57f339` (2026-10-26)

> ### H-1. Time hand-refill effects for when your hand naturally runs out
> - **Claim:** "Draw until you have N" effects (e.g. Demona – Scourge of the Wyvern Clan, refill to 3) are worth
>   most on the turn your hand empties. Playing one card per turn on the play, a 7-card hand empties around
>   turn 6, which is exactly when a 6-cost refill lands. So an inkable refill card is cheap to keep through a
>   mulligan: if the game goes differently, ink it.
> - **Why:**
>   - **Hand count when playing on curve, going first:** 7 cards; ink 1 and play 1 each turn, drawing from turn 2,
>     leaves 1 card after inking on turn 6.
>   - **Cost of keeping it, by simulation:** keeping Demona costs about 0.1 ink spent over turns 1–4, compared with
>     also mulliganing her.
> - **Caveats:**
>   - "Each player" refills, so the opponent also draws if below N. On the draw they hold one more card.
>   - Demona's STONE BY DAY means she can't ready while you hold 3+ cards. After refilling to 3, you must be at ≤2
>     by your next Ready step, or she stays exerted (and challengeable) once she has quested or challenged.
>   - She's drying on the turn she's played, so her own exert-all opens the opponent's board to your *other* dry
>     characters only.
>   - Ink drops (Hyperia City) don't expire, so saved drops can bring the refill earlier than the bare count suggests.
>   - **The cap absorbs earlier card draw.** "Draw until 3" always ends at 3, so cards drawn beforehand (e.g. Junior
>     Woodchuck Guidebook) are borrowed from the refill rather than added to it. Pre-drawing buys timing, not
>     cards:
>     - earlier information
>     - a better ink choice
>     - being sure of an inkable card for the refill turn
>
>     Weigh that against what the same ink would otherwise buy, e.g. an earlier body. (User's correction,
>     2026-10-24: the coach had called this a cost of the draw without counting those gains.)
>   - The opponent's refill depends on *their* hand size when you cast it, not on your earlier choices. Check it
>     when deciding whether to cast now.
>   - **Against Amber/Sapphire**, Demona's 5 {S} makes her a target for Gaston – Superior Archer, Judy Hopps –
>     Always Vigilant and World's Greatest Criminal Mind (`kb/meta/archetypes/amber-sapphire.md`).
> - **Evidence for:**
>   - replay 01a14bf9 (vs Goop), 2026-10-24, pre-game: the user's mulligan reasoning.
>   - same game, turn 5: the user kept Cheshire as the bridge play on the way to Demona instead of playing an
>     extra 1-drop.
>   - same game, turn 9: Guidebook played and used before inking, which locked in an inkable card for Demona
>     on turn 11. The alternative, Cheshire plus Boost, had about a 16% chance (8 uninkables in 49 cards) of an
>     uninkable turn-11 draw blocking her.
>   - same game, turn 11 (agreed with the user): a second Cheshire instead of Boost would have developed the
>     board, used the ink better, and **emptied the hand to just Demona**. Her next-turn refill would then draw 3
>     instead of 1. The user's own summary of the lesson: develop the board and spend resources so the
>     refill draws as many cards as possible.
>   - same game, turn 13: with Demona due next turn, the user inked their spare Cheshire though that ink wasn't
>     needed. **Corollary:** when a refill is imminent, spare cards in hand are effectively free ink. The cap
>     replaces them, so inking them costs no cards and leaves Demona as the only card in hand.
>   - **Limit on that corollary** (same game, turn 15, agreed with the user): it's free in card *count* only. The cap
>     replaces the inked card with a *random* card. So it holds for cards no better than a random draw, but not
>     for engines or must-answer threats. Inking Dumbo – Ninth Wonder of the Universe (a draw-and-lore engine the
>     opponent has to answer, TEMPO-1) to top up Demona's refill from 2 to 3 was probably wrong. Better was to
>     cast Demona without inking, keep Dumbo for next turn, and accept 1 ink and 1 refill card less.
> - **Evidence against:** none yet.
> - **Status:** open.

> ### H-3. Use repeatable effects as a reaction, not as pre-emptive deterrence
> - **Claim:** If an effect can be used again on a later turn (once-per-turn Boost triggers, activated abilities),
>   don't spend it now just to deter something the opponent *might* do. Wait until they commit, then use it to
>   punish. Using it after their action is usually just as effective. Using it beforehand spends resources (ink,
>   a card under, the effect itself) on something that may never happen, and may feed their deck.
> - **Why:** SEQ-1 (delay irreversible decisions) applied to deterrence. Reacting also uses information you
>   don't have yet.
> - **Doesn't apply when:** the reaction would come too late, i.e. what you're deterring wins the game or can't
>   be undone. Or the effect can't be used again in time.
> - **Evidence for (weak):** replay 01a14bf9 (vs Goop), turn 11. Cheshire Cat's Boost moved 2 damage onto Bruno
>   to deter a Bruno-into-Jebidiah challenge. The same move after that challenge would have finished Bruno
>   anyway, and damage on Bruno fed their healing engine. **But the main lesson of that turn was board development,
>   resource use and setting up the Demona refill (see H-1). Reactive use was a secondary point.**
> - **Evidence against:** none yet.
> - **Evidence for:** same game, turn 19. The user held a second Demona – Imperious Spellcaster instead of
>   playing her into an empty board. Her IMPETUS IMPROVISUS (discard a card) can give *herself* Rush and Evasive,
>   so held back she can land later and challenge an exerted quester the same turn: a surprise. Playing her
>   early gained little:
>   - Questing on turn 21 would have exposed her to their 4-{S} characters.
>   - She'd have had nothing to challenge, since their new characters would be ready.
>
>   Agreed with the user as a reasonable line, and a cleaner fit for this hypothesis: *hold a threat to deploy
>   after the opponent commits*. The cost: IMPETUS needs a discard, so it uses two cards.
> - **User's view (2026-10-24):** too generalised to promote. Needs examples where reactive use is the main point.
>   (The turn-19 example above is one candidate; not yet reviewed with the user as evidence for H-3.)
> - **Status:** open.

> ### Raging Storm (Amber action, 8, uninkable)
> **Text:** "Banish all characters." (verified, replay 01a14bf9)
>
> **How common:** lots of Amber decks run 1–3 copies (user, 2026-10-25, Set 14 era).
>
> **Hard to play around.** The practical response is not to overcommit once an Amber opponent reaches 8 ink:
> - Keep a threat or a refill in hand.
> - Prefer lines that remove their cards without adding many of yours (POS-1).
>
> **Chance they've seen at least one copy** in a 60-card deck:
>
> | Cards seen | 1 copy | 2 copies | 3 copies |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 12 | 20% | 36% | 49% |
> | 16 | 27% | 47% | 61% |
> | 20 | 33% | 56% | 71% |
> | 24 | 40% | 64% | 79% |
> | 28 | 47% | 72% | 86% |
>
> Count opening hand, mulligan redraws, draws, and extra cards looked at or drawn. Subtract any copies already
> seen. Around turn 14, a typical opponent has seen about 20 cards.
>
> **After one copy has been cast**, a second is much less likely in any given turn:
> - **Example (01a14bf9, turn 17):** about 8% if they run 2, 15% if 3, assuming the 3 cards available to them
>   next turn (2 in hand + 1 draw) are random among 39 unseen.
> - **It did come, on turn 18.** Low-probability events happen, so judge the commitment by the odds, not the result.
>
> **Pattern seen:** the caster quests with their own characters *first* to bank their lore, then wipes
> (01a14bf9, turn 18: +4 lore, then Raging Storm). Expect the wipe after their quests, not before.

> ### "Whenever one of your characters is banished" sees its own source; bagged triggers outlive their source
> - **Situation:** Mulan – Elite Archer (shifted, 5 {S}) challenged an exerted Isabela Madrigal while the opponent had
>   Belle – Snowfield Strategist in play. Belle's WINTER STOCKPILE: "Whenever one of your characters is banished, you
>   may put that card from your discard into your inkwell facedown and exerted." TRIPLE SHOT then dealt 5 to Belle.
> - **Ruling:**
>   1. Isabela is banished at the game state check after challenge damage, while Belle is in play, so WINTER
>      STOCKPILE triggers.
>   2. The active player's TRIPLE SHOT resolves first and banishes Belle.
>   3. Belle's ability sees her own banishing, because she's one of "your characters", and triggers again.
>   4. Both triggers resolve even though Belle has left play.
>
>   The opponent ramps +2. Killing both can't avoid the ramp: Belle was ready, so she could only be hit by

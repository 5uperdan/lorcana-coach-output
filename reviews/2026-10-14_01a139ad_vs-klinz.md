## Lessons
1. **Ink-short hands change the mulligan.** With uninkables in hand, the 6-drop refill is the card to send back,
   not the cheap curve ([H-1](#cite-h-1) caveat).
2. **Opposing characters are free singers** ([RULE-9](#cite-rule-9)). Tod, Sven and Milo between them sang three songs for free in
   this game. Count their 3+ / 5+ cost characters when they have Malicious, Mean, and Scary or Above the Crowd.
3. **Against Emerald/Amethyst, expect the Malicious, Mean, and Scary + Prince Phillip wipe from 9 ink.** Don't
   deploy characters the turn before that unless they'll get value first. Rebuild right after (turn 25 did this
   well).
4. **Before digging because nothing looks good, run the race, and count their sweepers** ([H-4](#cite-h-4)).
5. **When choosing what to sacrifice, compare what's left on board and in hand afterwards** ([TRADE-1](#cite-trade-1)). A damaged
   1-drop is cheaper than a growing Scrooge.
6. **Dumbo timing:** play him with spare ink and a dry Evasive ally, so he pays off on arrival. Don't overpay for
   the setup. Against "3 {S} or less" removal, expect him to go quickly ([the Dumbo card note](#cite-kb-cards-dumbo-ninth-wonder-of-the-universe)).
7. **Ward blanks "chosen" exerts** (Isis, Shadow). Effects that don't choose, like Demona – Scourge's exert-all, still work.
8. **Extra cards matter less to an opponent with a full hand and limited ink.** That's when "let them draw 2" over
   losing a body is most acceptable ([H-2](#cite-h-2) converse).

> ### H-1. Time hand-refill effects for when your hand naturally runs out
> - **Claim:** "Draw until you have N" effects (e.g. Demona – Scourge of the Wyvern Clan, refill to 3) are worth
>   most on the turn your hand empties. Playing one card per turn on the play, a 7-card hand empties around
>   turn 6, which is exactly when a 6-cost refill lands. So an inkable refill card is cheap to keep through a
>   mulligan: if the game goes differently, ink it.
> - **Why:**
>   - **Hand count when playing on curve, going first:** 7 cards; ink 1 and play 1 each turn, drawing from turn 2,
>     leaves 1 card after inking on turn 6.
>   - **Cost of keeping it, by simulation:** keeping Demona costs about 0.1 ink spent over turns 1–4, compared with
>     also mulliganing her.
> - **Caveats:**
>   - "Each player" refills, so the opponent also draws if below N. On the draw they hold one more card.
>   - Demona's STONE BY DAY means she can't ready while you hold 3+ cards. After refilling to 3, you must be at ≤2
>     by your next Ready step, or she stays exerted (and challengeable) once she has quested or challenged.
>   - She's drying on the turn she's played, so her own exert-all opens the opponent's board to your *other* dry
>     characters only.
>   - Ink drops (Hyperia City) don't expire, so saved drops can bring the refill earlier than the bare count suggests.
>   - **The cap absorbs earlier card draw.** "Draw until 3" always ends at 3, so cards drawn beforehand (e.g. Junior
>     Woodchuck Guidebook) are borrowed from the refill rather than added to it. Pre-drawing buys timing, not
>     cards:
>     - earlier information
>     - a better ink choice
>     - being sure of an inkable card for the refill turn
>
>     Weigh that against what the same ink would otherwise buy, e.g. an earlier body. (User's correction,
>     2026-10-24: the coach had called this a cost of the draw without counting those gains.)
>   - The opponent's refill depends on *their* hand size when you cast it, not on your earlier choices. Check it
>     when deciding whether to cast now.
>   - **It needs your ink to reach 6 on time.** Hands with uninkable cards are short of ink, not cards, so a held
>     6-drop is rarely castable on turn 6.
>     - **01a139ad, pre-game:** an opening seven with two uninkables. Keeping Demona left her castable on turn 6
>       only ~16% of the time in simulation. Mulliganing her for a random card raised "turn 4 uses all ink" from
>       80% to 91%. In ink-short hands, the refill card is the one to send back.
>     - *This caveat is the coach's, from simulation; not yet discussed with the user.*
>   - **Against Amber/Sapphire**, Demona's 5 {S} makes her a target for Gaston – Superior Archer, Judy Hopps –
>     Always Vigilant and World's Greatest Criminal Mind (`kb/meta/archetypes/amber-sapphire.md`).
> - **Evidence for:**
>   - replay 01a14bf9 (vs Goop), 2026-10-24, pre-game: the user's mulligan reasoning.
>   - same game, turn 5: the user kept Cheshire as the bridge play on the way to Demona instead of playing an
>     extra 1-drop.
>   - same game, turn 9: Guidebook played and used before inking, which locked in an inkable card for Demona
>     on turn 11. The alternative, Cheshire plus Boost, had about a 16% chance (8 uninkables in 49 cards) of an
>     uninkable turn-11 draw blocking her.
>   - same game, turn 11 (agreed with the user): a second Cheshire instead of Boost would have developed the
>     board, used the ink better, and **emptied the hand to just Demona**. Her next-turn refill would then draw 3

> ### H-2. Spare ink isn't automatically wasted when later turns will be short of cards
> - **Claim:** Ink grows by one every turn, but cards don't. Once later turns are likely to have more ink than
>   good cards to spend it on, cards are the scarcer resource. Then playing a low-impact card just to use spare
>   ink (giving up a better card to the inkwell instead) is usually worse than leaving the ink unspent.
> - **Applies when:** you're ahead on board (POS-1) and the extra play has little impact (WIN-2). Also when the
>   backup value of the extra card is low, e.g. a second Shift target when the opponent's removal can't hit the first.
> - **Doesn't apply when:** you're behind and need board presence now, or the cheap card has real impact.
> - **The converse, for the opponent:** extra cards are worth less to an opponent who already has a full hand and is
>   short of ink. When an effect offers "they draw 2 or you lose a body" (Hades – Looking for a Deal), keeping
>   the body is more attractive the more cards they already hold. Discard-payoff decks still get some fuel from
>   extra cards. Evidence: 01a139ad, turn 14. The opponent had 5 cards and 7 ink, and the user kept Isis
>   rather than bottoming her. Discussed 2026-10-26 and judged a close call.
> - **Evidence for:** replay 01a14bf9 (vs Goop), turn 5. The user left 1 ink unspent rather than ink Cheshire to
>   play a second Mulan – Injured Soldier. Coach first called it a close call, then agreed after the user's argument.
> - **Evidence against:** none yet.
> - **Status:** open.

> ### H-4. Before digging because no option looks good, check the race
> - **Claim:** When none of the plays look appealing, it's tempting to spend the turn on card draw "to find a
>   plan". First run the lore arithmetic (QC-5): count turns to 20 for both sides with each candidate play. A plain
>   quester that wins the race by a turn is already a plan. Digging is right when no line wins or holds the race.
> - **Why:** card draw feels productive, but a turn without board development pushes your clock back a turn.
>   Against a deck that out-draws you, the draw rarely catches up (ROLE-1: you're the aggressor).
> - **Evidence for:**
>   - 01a139ad, turn 19: card draw (Guidebook, The Torn Corner) chosen over Wasabi.
>   - 01a139ad, turn 21: Guidebook plus an Isis whose ability had no target, chosen over Wasabi. Race maths: Wasabi
>     meant winning on turn 27, a turn before them; Isis meant turn 29, a turn after.
>
>   The user's explanation for turn 21: "didn't really like any of the options, so favoured card draw to find a
>   good plan".

>   - That hand was all uninkable, and digging found an ink card (Pepita), which does have value.
>   - Digging is also right when the race is already lost on the current plan.

> # Dumbo – Ninth Wonder of the Universe
>
> Amethyst, 4-cost, 3/3/1, Evasive (text verified from replay data):
> - **BREAKING RECORDS:** {E}, 1 {I} — Draw a card and gain 1 lore.
> - **MAKING HISTORY:** Your other characters with Evasive gain "{E}, 1 {I} — Draw a card and gain 1 lore."
>
> ## Timing
> - **Dumbo is drying the turn he's played**, so he can't use his own ability that turn (CR 1.7.5).
> - **Your *other* Evasive characters that are already dry can use the granted ability immediately.** So the turn
>   he lands, his value is one draw-and-lore per dry Evasive ally and per spare ink.
> - **Play him when you have spare ink and a dry Evasive ally**, not as an all-in 4-drop that uses all your ink. In
>   01a139ad, turn 7, the user held him for a turn with 5 ink, so 4 for Dumbo plus 1 for an activation the same
>   turn. Agreed after discussion (2026-10-26).
>
> - **Don't overpay for the setup.** Deploying a cheap Evasive ally a turn early, just so Dumbo pays off on landing,
>   costs tempo if it leaves ink unused. Compare it with simply playing your strongest card. In 01a139ad,
>   turn 15, the user played Jebidiah (2) for the Dumbo setup and left 3 ink idle. Wasabi (5) was agreed to be
>   better: 2 lore a turn, robust against pings and Shere Khan, and it uses all the ink (2026-10-26).
>
> ## Synergies
> - **Reckless Evasive characters** (Jebidiah Farnsworth – Cookie) can't quest but *can* exert for the granted
>   ability (CR 8.7.4). With Dumbo, a 0-lore Reckless body becomes a draw-and-lore engine.
> - **Other Evasive characters in the user's list:** Peter Pan's Shadow – Elusive Prankster; Demona – Imperious
>   Spellcaster (via her own IMPETUS); Ursula – Whisper of Vanessa (while there's a card under her).
>
> ## Risks
> - **Must-answer** (TEMPO-1). He's only 3 {W}.
> - **3 {S} puts him in range of "3 {S} or less" removal.** Against Emerald/Amethyst, Shere Khan – Ruthless Entrepreneur
>   and Above the Crowd (sung free by their 5+ cost characters) bottomed both of the user's Dumbos in 01a139ad
>   (turns 12 and 18). Against that deck, expect him to be answered quickly. Use the activation the turn he
>   lands, so he has paid something before he goes.
> - **Exerting to activate exposes the activator.** Opposing Evasive characters (e.g. Sven – Leaping Reindeer with
>   Rush) can challenge exerted Evasive characters.
